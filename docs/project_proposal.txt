ECE1508 Project Proposal

Abstract:
The goal of this project is to use deep reinforcement learning (DRL) to train a robotic arm and an end-effector, or a net in this case, to catch a ball being thrown toward it. Rather than explicitly predicting the balls’ trajectory, the agent learns a policy that repeatedly observes the ball and moves the net, so the ball passes through it. In reinforcement learning terms, the agent in this case is the robot, and its observations are the ball’s position and velocity as provided from the ground truth of the simulator with Gaussian noise added to the ball position to mimic a camera-based tracker, and the joint positions, joint velocities, and orientation of the of the catching net. Based on these observations, the agent will take actions that involve defining the displacement of the hoop (from neutral position) in Cartesian coordinates, which will be converted to joint commands through inverse kinematics. The reward it gets are defined by the hoop’s proximity to the ball and its alignment with the ball’s direction of travel if the ball is not caught, and a large reward when the ball passes through the ring, and penalties for rim contact. The policy will be trained with randomized throws from different angles, so it generalizes across trajectories. 
This project will be demonstrated entirely in simulation using MuJoCo. The physics-based environment will feature a robot arm with a fixed base and be configured to throw simulated balls with varying initial positions, velocities, and trajectories, all of which will land in the arm’s reachable area. 
To achieve the goals outlined above, we will use Catch_It, an open-source implementation of a learning-based catching system presented at ICRA 2025, as the starting codebase. It provides a MuJoCo simulation of a mobile manipulator: a wheeled based, a 6-DoF xArm6 arm, and a hand. It also includes randomized object throws, and an inverse-kinematics and PID control stack for moving the end effector. It also contains a PyTorch implementation of Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) with training and evaluation scripts, which we will adapt to our task. We will freeze the mobile base, replace the hand with a rigid hoop-and-net end-effector, redesign the throw sampler so the throws land within the arm’s reach, and replace palm-contact with ring-crossing as a success condition, and design a new reward function.
The outputs will include a trained DRL policy, a simulated demonstration of the robotic arm catching the ball using the policy and an evaluation of its performance against a baseline. The baseline fits a parabola to the observed ball trajectory and moves the net to the predicted intercept point. The catching success rate across varying different ball trajectories will be used to assess the generalization of the learned policy. The codebase will also be uploaded on GitHub.
The requirements for this project include a computer capable of running the simulation and training workloads, a starting codebase (Catch_It), and training data. Because Catch It runs MuJoCo physics on the CPU and its policy uses state observations from ground truth provided by the simulator rather than images, laptops are sufficient, and no GPU is required. To get training data, a ball will be spawned at different starting points in front of the robot and thrown at different launch angles to a goal point that is within the arm’s reachable space. A harder set with a fixed seed will also be made to evaluate the learned policy against the baseline.
